DBS Bank: Governance RACI for Recurring Transformation Decisions

Seven decisions that will come back again and again as the eight roadmap initiatives move from Crawl to Walk to Run. Each has exactly one Accountable role, and each is tied to a decision point on the roadmap.

RACI matrix

Rows are decisions, not tasks. Columns are DBS-style executive roles. Every row has one A.

A Accountable: one per row, owns the outcome and cannot delegate it. R Responsible: does the work. C Consulted: gives input before the decision. I Informed: told after, no input rights. Role assignments are our team's proposal; DBS does not publish a RACI.

Why each Accountable, and where it bites on the roadmap

For each decision: the Accountable rationale, the roadmap decision point that creates it (initiative names match the roadmap), and what in the system would make it true when nobody is looking.

1 · Budget allocation A: Group CFO

Decision
How transformation funding is divided across the eight initiatives, and when it is re-cut, for example funding Technology Insourcing and Cloud-native Infrastructure first while holding back money for digibank Indonesia until digibank India proves out.
Why this Accountable
The CFO controls capital allocation and is the one role that sees every business case against the bank's total return. A business head as owner would let one unit's P&L tilt the split, and the CIO as owner would make the largest recipient the judge of its own budget.
Roadmap decision point
Crawlfunding for Technology Insourcing, Cloud-native Infrastructure, ADA Data Platform · Walk→Runrelease of funds for Platform Operating Model and digibank Indonesia.
Enforcement (proposed)
Funds released in tranches, each only after a CFO-signed gate.

2 · Architecture standards A: Group Head, Technology & Operations (CIO)

Decision
Whether a team may depart from the target cloud-native, API-first architecture for speed or cost, and who approves exceptions that touch the shared platform.
Why this Accountable
The CIO owns the shared technology stack and answers for outages or lock-in if an exception goes wrong. Platform teams are Responsible for following and proposing standards, but should not approve their own exceptions.
Roadmap decision point
CrawlCloud-native Infrastructure sets the standard · Walkdigibank India and API Developer Platform each pressure it for faster releases.
Enforcement (proposed)
An exception must be logged and approved before a release can go to production.

3 · Data governance A: Chief Data Officer

Decision
Who owns data quality and access policy on the ADA Data Platform when business units want faster or wider access than the rules allow.
Why this Accountable
ADA is meant to be one governed source of truth, which only holds if a single role owns the rules across every unit. The CDO sits above any one unit's incentives. The Chief Risk Officer is Consulted because access rules carry privacy and regulatory risk.
Roadmap decision point
CrawlADA Data Platform (governance design) · WalkCustomer Journey Thinking in Retail Lending needs trusted data to measure "Time to Yes."
Enforcement (proposed)
All data access requests go through one CDO-owned approval workflow.

4 · Initiative prioritization A: Group CEO

Decision
When engineers and funds are limited, which initiative is built next and which is deferred, for example which Walk initiative gets scarce engineers first.
Why this Accountable
Prioritization trades one unit's needs against another's and against the enterprise architecture, so it has to sit above all of them. Only the CEO can make that call stick. The CIO and the Consumer Banking head are Responsible because they supply the capacity and the demand.
Roadmap decision point
Crawl→Walkgate: sequencing of Customer Journey Thinking, digibank India and API Developer Platform · Walk→Rungate.
Enforcement (proposed)
One ranked backlog reviewed at every QBR; it can only be re-ordered by the CEO.

5 · Vendor and partnership decisions A: Group Head, Technology & Operations (CIO)

Decision
Which external vendors and partners are approved and governed, including when a vendor is still justified while capability is being insourced, and which partners may build on the API Developer Platform.
Why this Accountable
Vendors and API partners both touch the security and architecture of the shared platform, which the CIO is accountable for. Insourcing was a deliberate move away from vendor dependency, so one technology owner should decide when a vendor is the exception. The Chief Risk Officer is Consulted on third-party risk.
Roadmap decision point
CrawlTechnology Insourcing (build versus buy) · WalkAPI Developer Platform (partner onboarding).
Enforcement (proposed)
No contract is signed and no partner is onboarded without a recorded CIO sign-off.

6 · Market launch gate A: Group Head, Consumer Banking case-specific

Decision
When a digibank market launch proceeds to the next market, and how much investment is released for it.
Why this Accountable
The Consumer Banking head owns the customer business digibank serves and answers for whether a launch earned its place. The go decision rests on the Walk readiness outcome (digibank India's customer milestone), so the owner of that outcome should own the decision. The CFO and Chief Risk Officer are Consulted before it is made.
Roadmap decision point
Walkdigibank India readiness outcome → Rundigibank Indonesia.
Enforcement (proposed)
The next market cannot start until the readiness outcome is met and signed off by this role.

7 · Co-leadership conflicts and shared KPIs A: Group CEO case-specific

Decision
When a platform's business and technology co-leads disagree, such as a business unit's near-term P&L against the enterprise roadmap, who decides, and what the two are jointly measured on.
Why this Accountable
"Two-in-a-box" splits leadership by design, so neither co-lead can break a tie between their own function's interests. Only the CEO sits above both the business and technology lines. Co-leads are Responsible for agreeing jointly first and escalating only the trade-offs they cannot resolve.
Roadmap decision point
RunPlatform Operating Model (shared platform KPIs and joint execution roadmap, per the roadmap's citation of DBS Annual Report 2018).
Enforcement (proposed)
Shared KPIs mean neither co-lead's scorecard improves by overriding the other. Unresolved items escalate to the CEO at the QBR.

Coverage and handoffs

  • Required decision types: budget (1), architecture (2), data governance (3), prioritization (4), vendors and partnerships (5), plus two case-specific decisions (6, 7).
  • Concentration to watch: the CEO is Accountable for two rows and the CIO for two. We kept the CEO to the two decisions that cross business and technology lines; if the CEO becomes the default escalation for everything, the matrix has failed.
  • For the dashboard's "Decisions Required This Quarter": take one to three rows from this table. Decision 4 (Crawl→Walk and Walk→Run gates), decision 6 (the India → Indonesia gate) and decision 7 (Platform Operating Model) are the ones the roadmap forces first.
  • verification Role titles are modeled on DBS's executive structure and must be checked against the DBS Annual Report leadership pages before submission. Where DBS uses a different title, we use DBS's. The public record does not show who actually held each decision at DBS, so these assignments are our design, not DBS's reported practice.
